Where to get a licensed copy of MS-DOS

Version dated 18 November 2026.

MBFUTool ships no MS-DOS files: they belong to Microsoft and cannot be redistributed with the program. You bring your own MS-DOS image — here is how to obtain one lawfully and what to watch out for.

Why MS-DOS is not included

MS-DOS 5.00 and 6.22 are proprietary Microsoft operating systems. Support ended in 2001, you cannot buy a new copy from Microsoft, and there is no official free download. MBFUTool only works with images: it clones media, writes partitions and edits files. The system files are yours to supply, as with any imaging utility.

Option 1: a Visual Studio subscription (formerly MSDN)

The cleanest route is Microsoft's subscriber catalogue. Open my.visualstudio.com/downloads and sign in with the account your Visual Studio subscription is tied to (Professional, Enterprise or a corporate subscription).

  • Search the catalogue for MS-DOS; DOS images appear among the operating systems.
  • Availability depends on the subscription level: if MS-DOS is not listed, your subscription does not include it — check another level or ask your company's subscription administrator.
  • You get an ISO or an archive of installation files — exactly what MBFUTool needs.
  • Subscription terms allow development and testing use, not resale. For a production machine a separate licence for the operating system is cleaner.

Option 2: a copy you already own

Original floppies, a boxed copy or a computer with MS-DOS preinstalled is a lawful copy. Under the first-sale doctrine (US), the EU exhaustion rule and art. 1272 of the Russian Civil Code, a lawfully acquired copy may be resold without the rights holder's consent.

  • Check completeness: disks, licence agreement, product number label.
  • OEM copies shipped with a PC are tied to that machine and formally cannot be moved to other hardware.
  • Before buying second-hand media, make sure the disks still read: 30-year-old floppies often have a rotted coating.

Option 3: image your own media

If MS-DOS already runs on an old machine or sits on floppies, make an image — MBFUTool works with that.

  • Floppies: read them on a working drive and save an IMG/IMA image.
  • Hard disk: image the whole partition while the system still boots, or from bootable media.
  • Check the result in DOSBox — it ships with MBFUTool.

Step-by-step instructions (in Russian): “Writing a DOS image with dd and Win32DiskImager”.

What not to do

  • Downloading “MS-DOS 6.22 ISO” from file-sharing or warez sites: that is counterfeit, even if you later buy something from the utility's author.
  • Using someone else's serial numbers or cracks.
  • Redistributing MS-DOS images, including “for testing” on forums.

Infringement exposes you to damages or statutory compensation and, depending on the jurisdiction, administrative or criminal liability.

What next

With an MS-DOS image at hand, start MBFUTool: pick an operation, point it at the image and the target device. The program refuses to write to the system disk and asks for confirmation — everything on the USB stick will be erased.

Quick answers

Can MS-DOS be downloaded for free legally?

Microsoft offers no free download. MS-DOS comes with a Visual Studio subscription or with a copy you already own.

I still have original MS-DOS floppies — is that enough?

Yes, provided the copy was lawfully acquired. Image the floppies and point the program at the image.

Is an image from the internet fine?

Only if it comes from the rights holder or through a subscription. An image downloaded from a file-sharing site is counterfeit, and the user bears the consequences.

What if MS-DOS is needed for a commercial service?

Then you need your own operating-system licence, not a development subscription. MBFUTool itself imposes no limit on commercial use, but it is licensed per computer: there are no corporate terms, invoices or bank transfers, and the only way to buy is Boosty.
